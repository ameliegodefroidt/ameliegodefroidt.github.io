##### Abstract

Why are some individuals more willing than others to engage in political violence? Drawing on research on the quest for significance and sociological work on anomia, this study examines whether feelings of meaninglessness, powerlessness, isolation, self-estrangement, and normlessness are associated with violent political intentions. Using data from 1,240 undergraduate students across Brazil, Turkey, Belgium, and France, we find a consistent positive association between anomia and intentions to engage in political violence. The relationship is small to moderate in size, emerges across all four countries, and remains present irrespective of political ideology. These findings point to anomia as a potentially important psychological and sociological factor in understanding violent political behavior.
